What Is a 4D Ultrasound?

A plain-English explainer on how 4D ultrasound works, how it differs from 2D and 3D, and what to expect at your session.

Published January 10, 2026 · 5 min read

If you've only ever seen a 2D ultrasound picture — the grainy, black-and-white cross-section most routine prenatal scans use — a 4D ultrasound can feel like a completely different experience. Instead of a flat still image, 4D ultrasound adds the fourth dimension of time to a 3D image, producing a moving, video-like view of your baby inside the womb.

In practice, that means you can watch your baby yawn, stretch, suck a thumb, or turn their head in real time, rather than trying to interpret a single still frame. For many parents, especially those meeting a first child, this is the moment pregnancy starts to feel real in a new way.

4D ultrasound is generally considered an elective, keepsake-style scan rather than a diagnostic medical test. Your regular prenatal care and diagnostic ultrasounds should continue with your primary OB provider — a 4D session is a complement to that care, not a replacement for it.

Image quality depends on a few factors: the baby's position, the amount of amniotic fluid, the thickness of the abdominal wall, and gestational age. That's why timing your session well (more on that in our next article) makes a real difference in what you'll see.

At Family Tree Oki, every 4D session is explained in plain English from booking through the scan itself, so you can focus on the experience rather than the technical details.
